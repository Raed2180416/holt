Contrare Research — offset halves
Umbrella · Institute
Contrare Research
A square split in half — the top shifts left, the bottom shifts right. The halves disagree. The form is against itself: two parts of the same shape that refuse to align. That tension IS the mark. You can draw it from memory: two offset bars. It reads as a fault line, a tectonic shift, a structure under contrarian stress.
ARC — the arc
Research core
ARC
A bold arc — 270 degrees of a thick ring, with a gap at the top right. The name made geometric. An arc is a curve, a trajectory, a path through space. The gap is the opening — where the research extends, where the work continues. It's the most literal mark in the system and that's its strength: you see it and you see ARC. Nobody else owns a thick arc.
CLIDE — block with chevron cut
Interface · Tool
CLIDE
A solid block with a bold chevron cut from its right side. The block is the interface — the IDE, the window, the container. The chevron is the command prompt — the CLI, the instruction, the thing you type. One form, two meanings: the command eating into the interface, the CLI inside the IDE. The chevron gives the block direction and energy — it's not static, it's a tool in motion.

Medes

Holt — lock shackle (unchanged)

Holt
